I direct AI video like a film shoot.

Every shot gets a written brief: framing, one action, one camera move. Faces, costumes and places are locked with reference images, so the same character survives every cut. These films were rendered on four Mac minis wired together over Thunderbolt.

In production: The City Without Time

A three-minute silent sci-fi fantasy short. At noon the clock tower of a brass city stops, and everyone freezes except a young clockmaker and the mechanical sparrow she built. 28 shots, seven title cards, one score. These three reference sheets lock the cast and the world for every shot.

How I work

  1. Story first, then a shot listA beat sheet becomes numbered shots. Each shot does one thing: one action, one camera move, a change of size from the shot before.
  2. Reference-locked continuityCharacter and location sheets are generated first and passed into every shot, so faces, costumes and places stay on model across hard cuts.
  3. Written like a camera briefEach prompt leads with the shot: distance, camera height, which side we see, and the move. Static shots are stated as locked-off, so the model never drifts.
  4. Review, log, reshootEvery clip is scored against its brief. What the model gets wrong goes into a directing log that shapes the next brief.
